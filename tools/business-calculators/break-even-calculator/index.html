What Is a Break Even Calculator?

A break even calculator tells you the point where your business stops losing money. It is the number of units, or the amount of sales, you need each month so that what you earn covers everything you spend. Every sale after that point is profit.

The idea is simple. Some costs stay the same however much you sell, like rent, salaries and software. Others rise with every sale, like stock, packing, shipping and payment fees. The gap between your price and those per sale costs is your contribution, and you need enough contributions to pay for the fixed costs.

This calculator adds one thing most others leave out: your ad cost per sale. If you grow with Google or Meta ads, every order also carries an acquisition cost, and leaving it out makes your break even point look far easier to reach than it is.

How to Use the Break Even Calculator

  1. Enter your fixed costs per month. Add up rent, salaries, software, loan EMIs and anything else you pay whether you sell or not.
  2. Enter your selling price per unit before GST, since GST is not your income.
  3. Enter your variable cost per unit. Include product cost, packing, shipping, payment gateway charges and marketplace commission.
  4. Add your ad cost per sale if you pay for ads. Your CPA from Google Ads or Meta is the number to use.
  5. Add the units you expect to sell, a target profit and any one time investment to see profit, margin of safety and payback time.
  6. Read the tables to see how profit changes with volume, and how a small price change moves your break even point. Then copy the summary.

These are the standard cost volume profit formulas taught in accounting, as set out in the Xero guide to the break even point formula. Break even units are rounded up, because you cannot sell part of a unit.

Practical Tips

  • Small price moves matter a lot. In the sample, a 10% price rise cuts the units you need from 500 to 375. Check the price table before you run a discount.
  • Watch your ad cost per sale. If your CPA rises from ₹150 to ₹250, contribution falls to ₹200 and you need 750 units instead of 500.
  • Keep a margin of safety. Below 10%, one slow month puts you in the red. Aim for 20% or more.
  • Be honest with fixed costs. Include your own salary if you need one. A business that only breaks even without paying you is not really breaking even.
  • Use averages for many products. If you sell a range, use your average order value as the price and the average cost per order as the variable cost.
  • Your figures stay private. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is uploaded or saved.

Break Even and Your Marketing

Your break even point shows how much room your ads have. To bring the cost per sale down, start with our Google Ads optimization tips, and if you bid on value, read value based bidding in Google Ads explained. Selling more to the same visitors lowers your break even point too, so see our guide to conversion rate optimization. Frequently Asked Questions

1How do I calculate my break even point?

Subtract your variable cost per unit from your selling price to get your contribution per unit. Then divide your monthly fixed costs by that number. With ₹1,50,000 fixed costs, a ₹1,000 price and ₹700 of costs per sale, you need ₹1,50,000 divided by ₹300, which is 500 units a month.

2Should I count ad spend as a fixed or variable cost?

If you pay a fairly steady cost for each order, such as your CPA in Google Ads or Meta, treat it as a variable cost and enter it as ad cost per sale. If you spend a fixed monthly amount on branding or retainers whatever you sell, add it to fixed costs instead.

3What is margin of safety?

Margin of safety is how far your sales can fall before you make a loss. If you expect to sell 650 units and break even at 500, your margin of safety is 150 units, or about 23%. The higher it is, the safer your business is in a slow month.

4Why does the calculator say I can never break even?

That happens when each sale costs you as much as or more than you charge for it, once product costs and ad cost per sale are added. Selling more then only makes the loss bigger. You need to raise your price, cut costs per unit, or lower your ad cost per sale.
