What Is Customer Lifetime Value?

Customer lifetime value, often shortened to CLV or LTV, is the total profit you can expect from one customer over the whole time they buy from you. A customer who spends ₹1,200 today and comes back four times a year for three years is worth far more than that first order.

Knowing this number changes how you market. If you only look at the first sale, a ₹1,500 cost per customer looks like a loss. Once you see the full lifetime value, you know whether that spend pays back, how much more you could spend, and which lever grows profit fastest.

This calculator works for both kinds of business: shops and services where people buy again and again, and subscriptions where people pay every month until they cancel.

How to Use the Customer Lifetime Value Calculator

  1. Pick your model. Choose Repeat Purchases for ecommerce, restaurants, salons and most services. Choose Subscription for SaaS, memberships and monthly plans.
  2. Enter your customer numbers. For repeat purchases, add your average order value, orders per year and how many years a customer keeps buying. For subscriptions, add monthly revenue per customer and monthly churn.
  3. Enter your gross margin, the percent you keep after product, delivery and payment costs. Lifetime value is measured in profit, not sales.
  4. Add your cost to win a customer, your total ad and sales spend divided by new customers in the same period.
  5. Read the verdict and the levers table to see your LTV:CAC ratio, payback months and which change would add the most value. Then copy the summary.

These are the standard formulas used by finance and growth teams, as explained in HubSpot's guide to calculating customer lifetime value. A ratio near 3:1 is the most common benchmark for healthy growth.

What Is a Good LTV:CAC Ratio?

  • Below 1:1. You lose money on every new customer. Fix this before you spend more on ads.
  • 1:1 to 3:1. Profitable on paper, but thin once rent, salaries and returns are paid.
  • 3:1 to 5:1. Healthy. Each customer pays back their cost and funds growth.
  • Above 5:1. Very profitable, but you may be spending too little to grow. Test a higher budget.

Practical Tips

  • Use real data, not hopes. Take order value and repeat rate from your store or CRM for the last 12 months. For new businesses, keep years retained modest, such as 1 or 2.
  • Retention is usually the biggest lever. Check the levers table. Keeping customers one more year often adds more than a price rise.
  • Watch payback as well as the ratio. A 4:1 ratio that takes 20 months to pay back can still leave you short of cash.
  • Leave GST out. Enter prices before GST, since GST is not your income.
  • Your figures stay private. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded or saved.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How do I calculate customer lifetime value?

Multiply your average order value by the number of orders a customer places each year and by the number of years they stay. That gives lifetime revenue. Multiply it by your gross margin to get lifetime value in profit. For example, ₹1,200 times 4 orders times 3 years is ₹14,400, and at a 40% margin that is ₹5,760.

2How do I calculate CLV for a subscription business?

Divide your monthly gross profit per customer by your monthly churn rate. If customers pay ₹999 a month, you keep 40%, and 4% cancel each month, the average customer stays 25 months and is worth ₹999 times 0.4 divided by 0.04, which is ₹9,990.

3What is a good LTV to CAC ratio?

About 3:1 is the common benchmark, meaning a customer brings three times what you paid to win them. Below 1:1 you lose money on each customer. Well above 5:1 usually means you could spend more on marketing to grow faster.

4Should CLV use revenue or profit?

Use profit. Revenue makes every customer look more valuable than they are, because it ignores product, delivery and payment costs. This calculator shows both, but the LTV:CAC ratio and the highest safe CAC are based on gross profit.
