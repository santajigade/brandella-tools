What Changed and Why

What Is an Email List Cleaner?

An email list cleaner takes a raw list of addresses and turns it into one you can safely send to. Lists collected from forms, events, spreadsheets and old CRM exports are always messy: the same person appears twice, someone typed gmial.com, a few addresses are broken, and some sign ups used a throwaway inbox to grab a freebie.

Sending to those addresses costs you money on every campaign and hurts your sender reputation. Bounces and unengaged addresses tell Gmail and Yahoo that you do not manage your list, and the Google email sender guidelines ask you to keep spam complaints low and to send only to people who asked for your mail. Cleaning before every import is the cheapest fix there is.

How to Clean Your Email List

  1. Paste your list. Copy a column from Excel or Google Sheets, a whole CSV export, or even an email thread. Anything with an @ sign is picked up, and names and other columns are ignored.
  2. Choose your options. Typo fixes, Gmail matching and disposable removal are on by default. Turn on role address removal for newsletters.
  3. Review the table. Every change is listed with a reason, so you can check typo fixes before you trust them.
  4. Copy or download. Download the clean CSV for your email tool, or the full report to keep a record of what was removed.

Google confirms that dots do not matter in Gmail addresses, so priya.sharma and priyasharma reach the same inbox. Other providers treat dots as real, so the cleaner only merges Gmail.

Practical Tips

  • Clean before every import, not once a year. It takes seconds and stops bad addresses spreading into your CRM.
  • Keep the report. If someone asks why they stopped getting mail, the report shows exactly what happened to their address.
  • Remove hard bounces after each send. No browser tool can prove a mailbox exists. Your email platform's bounce list is the final word.
  • Drop people who never open. A valid address that ignores you for six months hurts deliverability too. Send a last check in email, then remove them.
  • Fix the source. If many sign ups come from throwaway inboxes, use double opt in on that form.

Frequently Asked Questions

1Does this tool check whether an email address really exists?

No. Checking a mailbox needs a connection to the mail server, which a browser cannot make and which would mean sending your list somewhere. This cleaner removes everything that can be caught locally: bad format, typos, duplicates, disposable and test addresses. Remove any address that hard bounces after your first send.

2Is my email list uploaded anywhere?

No. The cleaning runs entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Your list is never sent to a server, so you can safely clean customer data without sharing it with a third party.

3Why are Gmail addresses with dots or a plus treated as duplicates?

Gmail ignores dots in the part before the @ sign and delivers name+anything to the same inbox. So priya.sharma@gmail.com and priyasharma+offers@gmail.com belong to one person, and sending to both doubles your mail to them. You can turn this off if you need the versions kept apart.

4Should I remove role addresses like info@ and sales@?

For newsletters and marketing, usually yes. Role addresses are shared by a team, nobody personally signed up, and they tend to open less and complain more. For B2B leads or replies to enquiries they can be fine, which is why the cleaner only flags them unless you tick the option.
