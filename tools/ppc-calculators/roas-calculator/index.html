What Is ROAS?

ROAS stands for return on ad spend. It tells you how much revenue your ads brought in for every unit of money you spent on them. If you spend ₹10,000 and the ads drive ₹40,000 in sales, your ROAS is 4, usually written as 4x or 400%.

The formula is simple: ROAS = revenue from ads ÷ ad spend. Google Ads calls this conversion value per cost, and its Target ROAS bidding guide multiplies it by 100 to give a percent. That is why a target of 400% in Google Ads means 4x.

How to Use the ROAS Calculator

  1. Pick your currency and enter your total ad spend for the period.
  2. Enter the revenue from those ads. In Google Ads this is the conversion value column. In Meta Ads Manager it is purchase conversion value.
  3. Add your gross profit margin to see your break even ROAS and the real profit left after ad costs.
  4. Use the planner to turn a target ROAS into the revenue you need, or the most you can spend to reach a revenue goal.

What Is a Good ROAS?

There is no single good number. A ROAS of 3x is great for a store with a 60% margin and a loss for a store with a 25% margin. The right question is whether your ROAS is above your break even point. Work that out first, then set targets above it.

  • Low margin products such as electronics need a much higher ROAS than high margin products like cosmetics or digital goods.
  • Repeat buyers change the maths. If customers come back, a first order ROAS slightly under break even can still pay off over time.
  • Check attribution. Platforms often credit themselves for the same sale, so compare ad platform revenue with your own sales data.
  • Raise targets slowly. Jumping a Google Ads target ROAS too far at once can choke traffic.

Improve Your ROAS

To push ROAS up, cut wasted clicks with negative keywords, send real sale values with value based bidding, and read our Google Ads optimization tips. To find the exact ROAS you must beat, use the break even ROAS calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How do I calculate ROAS?

Divide the revenue your ads generated by what you spent on those ads. For example, ₹2,15,000 in revenue from ₹50,000 of spend is a ROAS of 4.3x, or 430%.

2What is the difference between ROAS and ROI?

ROAS compares revenue with ad spend only. ROI looks at profit, so it subtracts product costs and ad spend first. A campaign can have a healthy looking ROAS and still lose money if margins are thin.

3Why does Google Ads show ROAS as a percent?

Google Ads multiplies conversion value per cost by 100. So a target ROAS of 500% means you want 5 in sales for every 1 you spend on ads.

4Is my campaign data sent anywhere?

No. This ROAS calculator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded or stored.
