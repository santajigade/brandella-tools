What Is a Headline Analyzer?

A headline analyzer reads your title the way a busy reader and a search engine would, then scores it. It checks length, the mix of plain and vivid words, whether it sounds like clickbait, and where Google will cut it off. You get a score out of 100 and a short list of fixes, so you can improve a headline in a minute instead of guessing.

Your headline decides whether anyone reads the rest. It is the blue link in Google, the bold line on social media, and often the text an AI assistant shows when it cites your page. Google itself asks whether the main heading or page title provide a descriptive, helpful summary of the content in its guide to creating helpful content.

How to Use the Headline Analyzer

  1. Type your headline in the first box. The score and checks update as you type.
  2. Add your focus keyword if the page should rank for a phrase. The tool checks that it appears once, near the start.
  3. Work through the checks. Fix anything marked Fix first, then the Improve items.
  4. Look at the Google preview to see if the end of your title gets cut off.
  5. Paste several ideas into Compare Headlines to rank them, then copy the ranking as a CSV for your team.

The length and keyword rules follow Google's guide to title links, which says titles are cut to fit the device width and that repeating the same words adds nothing. The word balance targets are editorial guidelines, not fixed rules.

Practical Tips for Better Headlines

  • Write ten, keep one. Your first idea is rarely your best. Paste them all into the comparison and test the top two with real readers.
  • Be specific. 7 email mistakes that cost you sales beats some things about email. Numbers, names and results build trust.
  • Lead with what matters. Readers and search results notice the first few words most, so put the topic there.
  • Match the search. Questions and how to headlines mirror what people type into Google and ask AI assistants. Answer the question in your first paragraph.
  • Keep the title tag and H1 close. Google may use your main heading as the title link, so make both describe the same page.

Frequently Asked Questions

1What is a good headline score?

Aim for 70 or higher. A score of 80 or more means the headline has a clear length, a good word mix and no clickbait or keyword problems. A lower score is not a failure, it simply shows the quickest fixes, so use it as a guide and trust real reader data when you have it.

2How long should a headline be?

Most strong headlines use 6 to 13 words. For the title tag that shows in Google, stay within roughly 600 pixels, which is usually 50 to 60 characters. Google has no fixed length limit, but it cuts longer titles to fit the screen.

3What are power words and emotional words?

Power words make a clear promise or create urgency, such as proven, simple, mistakes or checklist. Emotional words name a feeling, such as love, fear, proud or struggle. One of each usually helps. Stacking many of them makes a headline sound like an advert.

4Is my headline sent anywhere?

No. The analyzer runs entirely in your browser. Headlines and keywords you type are never uploaded, stored or shared, so it is safe for unpublished work and client projects.
